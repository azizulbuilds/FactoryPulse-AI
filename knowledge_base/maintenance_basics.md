# Industrial Machine Maintenance Knowledge Base

## Purpose

This document provides general educational guidance for
interpreting industrial sensor anomalies.

It is not a replacement for equipment-specific manuals,
manufacturer instructions, or approved site procedures.

## 1. Understanding Temperature Anomalies

### What to observe

- Air temperature
- Process temperature
- Changes in temperature over time
- Differences between expected and observed readings

### Investigation guidance

An unusual temperature reading may justify checking:

- Sensor accuracy and calibration records
- Recent changes in operating conditions
- Cooling and ventilation conditions
- Equipment-specific temperature limits
- Relevant manufacturer troubleshooting instructions

### Important limitation

An unusual temperature reading does not independently
establish the cause of a machine problem.

Temperature limits must come from the applicable
manufacturer documentation and operating procedures.

## 2. Understanding Rotational Speed Anomalies

### What to observe

- Current rotational speed
- Changes from expected operating conditions
- Whether the machine is operating under a different load
- Whether the sensor reading is plausible

### Investigation guidance

Potential investigation areas include:

- Sensor readings and instrumentation
- Operating mode and commanded speed
- Recent changes in process conditions
- Equipment-specific operating limits
- Relevant manufacturer troubleshooting instructions

### Important limitation

An unusual rotational speed does not, by itself,
confirm a mechanical fault.

## 3. Understanding Torque Anomalies

### What to observe

- Measured torque
- Changes relative to normal operating conditions
- Simultaneous changes in rotational speed
- Whether the machine load has changed

### Investigation guidance

Potential investigation areas include:

- Changes in machine workload
- Operating conditions and process settings
- Sensor integrity
- Manufacturer-defined torque limits
- Relevant equipment maintenance records

### Important limitation

Torque changes must be interpreted in the context of
the machine, its operating mode, and its intended load.

## 4. Understanding Tool Wear

### What to observe

- Accumulated tool usage
- Maintenance and replacement records
- Changes in operating conditions
- Whether the recorded wear estimate is reliable

### Investigation guidance

Tool wear should be assessed according to the tool type,
manufacturer recommendations, operating conditions,
and the site's approved maintenance schedule.

An accumulated tool-wear measurement is not necessarily
a direct measurement of current physical wear.

## 5. Multiple Sensor Anomalies

When multiple measurements appear unusual at the same time,
review them together rather than interpreting each value
in isolation.

Useful context includes:

- Current operating mode
- Recent process changes
- Available historical readings
- Sensor quality and reliability
- Existing maintenance records
- Equipment-specific operating limits

Correlation between readings does not establish causation.

## 6. Safe Troubleshooting Principles

- Follow applicable site safety procedures.
- Consult the manufacturer's documentation.
- Use authorized personnel for inspection and maintenance.
- Apply required energy isolation and lockout/tagout
  procedures before work requiring them.
- Never bypass guards, interlocks, or safety systems.
- Do not perform energized inspections unless explicitly
  permitted under approved procedures by qualified personnel.
- Escalate uncertain or potentially hazardous conditions
  through the site's established safety process.

## 7. Reporting an Anomaly

A useful anomaly report should include:

1. The observed sensor readings.
2. The anomaly score and detection threshold, if available.
3. Which measurements appear unusual.
4. Relevant operating context.
5. Supporting maintenance documentation.
6. Suggested investigation steps grounded in the evidence.
7. Any safety limitations or information gaps.

## 8. Limitations of AI-Based Assessment

An anomaly score indicates how unusual a reading appears
according to a trained model.

It does not independently prove that a machine is failing.

An AI-generated explanation is not a confirmed diagnosis.

Maintenance recommendations must be supported by relevant
documentation and reviewed under the applicable operating
and safety procedures.
